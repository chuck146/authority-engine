## (c) Proposed new sections

### Solid vs. semi-transparent stain

**Semi-transparent stain**

- Shows the wood grain with a light tint of color
- Soaks into the wood instead of forming a surface film
- Wears away gradually, so it usually does not peel
- Easy to recoat: clean the deck and apply a fresh coat
- Does not hide stains, patched boards, or mismatched wood

**Solid stain**

- Covers the grain with an opaque color, like thin paint
- Hides weathering, stains, and mismatched boards
- Gives more UV protection to the wood underneath
- Forms a film that can peel on walking surfaces over time
- Hard to switch back to semi-transparent without stripping

**Rule of thumb:** Choose semi-transparent for newer or good-looking wood. Choose solid for older decks with visible wear or mixed boards. Railings are often a good place for solid, even when the floor gets semi-transparent.

### What affects price and timing

- Deck size, plus the number of stairs, railings, and spindles (spindles take the most hand work)
- The current finish. Stripping a peeling solid stain takes much more work than recoating a semi-transparent one.
- Wood condition: graying, mildew, splinters, raised grain
- The stain product chosen
- Board repair. Loose, cracked, or rotted boards are repaired or replaced by our crew before staining, and listed on your estimate.
- Weather. The deck must be dry before staining, and stain needs dry hours after.

Your free estimate gives you the price and an expected schedule for your deck.

### Before we arrive

- Move furniture, grills, planters, and rugs off the deck.
- Turn off sprinklers that hit the deck.
- Close nearby windows and keep pets inside on work days.
- Point out loose or damaged boards during the walkthrough so they are on the estimate.
